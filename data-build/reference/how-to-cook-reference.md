# How-to-cook reference (idiot-proof) — researched 2026-06-19

> **Liability label (use this exact framing in client-facing copy):** These are **suggestions, not requirements**. Cooking times vary by stove, pan, thickness, and starting temperature. **Always cook to the safe internal temperature with a food thermometer** — time is a guide, temperature is the truth.

## 🌡️ Safe minimum internal temperatures (FoodSafety.gov / USDA-FSIS — the law of the land)

The ONE rule that prevents food poisoning AND dryness: pull at the right temp, never "by the clock."

| Food | Safe minimum internal temp | Rest time |
|---|---|---|
| **Poultry** — chicken & turkey (breast, thigh, ground, whole) | **165°F** | none required |
| **Ground meat** — beef, pork, lamb, veal | **160°F** | none required |
| **Steaks / chops / roasts** — beef, pork, lamb, veal | **145°F** | **3 min** (the rest IS part of the safety step) |
| **Fresh ham (raw)** | **145°F** | 3 min |
| **Precooked ham (to reheat)** | **165°F** (or 140°F if USDA-inspected & resealed) | — |
| **Eggs** | cook until yolk & white are firm | — |
| **Egg dishes** (frittata, quiche, casserole) | **160°F** | — |
| **Fish (fillet or whole)** | **145°F** — or until opaque & flakes easily with a fork | — |
| **Shrimp, lobster, crab, scallops** | cook until flesh is **pearly/white and opaque** | — |
| **Clams, oysters, mussels** | cook until **shells open** during cooking (discard any that don't) | — |
| **Leftovers & casseroles** | **165°F** | — |

> **Carryover cooking — the pro move:** meat keeps cooking 5–15°F after it leaves the heat. **Pull below the target and let the rest finish it**, or you overshoot into rubber. See per-protein "pull temps" below.

## PROTEINS — per protein × method, with the #1 mistake and the dry/rubbery fix

### Why lean protein goes dry/rubbery (the science, once — applies to all of them)
Heat makes muscle proteins **denaturate and contract**, squeezing water out. The leaner the cut (chicken breast, shrimp, white fish, pork loin), the less fat buffers it, so a few extra minutes = leather. **Resting** lets fibers relax so they hold juice — a rested cut can lose up to ~40% less liquid when cut. So the universal fixes are: **(1) even thickness, (2) pull a few degrees early for carryover, (3) rest, (4) don't overcrowd the pan (crowding = steam = no sear).**

---

### CHICKEN BREAST (lean — dries fastest)
- **Pull temp:** 160°F, rest 5–8 min → carryover finishes it to the safe 165°F. (Pull AT 165 and you eat ~172°F = rubber.)
- **Pan-sear:** pound to even ~¾", pat dry, salt, medium-high oil, ~5–6 min/side, flip once. #1 mistake: uneven thickness → thin end rubber while thick end raw. Fix: pound flat or butterfly.
- **Bake/roast:** 425°F ~18–22 min (depends on thickness). #1 mistake: low-and-slow at 350°F too long dries it. Fix: hotter & shorter, pull at 160°F.
- **Air-fry:** 380°F ~10–12 min, flip halfway. Light oil so it doesn't go leathery.
- **Boil/poach:** gentle simmer (NOT rolling boil) ~12–15 min, off-heat finish in the hot water. Best for shredding; a hard boil seizes it tough.
- **Grill:** medium-high, ~5–6 min/side. Move off direct heat to finish. Keep-it-moist: brine 15–30 min, don't blow past 160°F.

### CHICKEN THIGH (forgiving — more fat & collagen)
- **Temp:** 165°F minimum, but thighs are best ~175–185°F — the extra heat melts collagen → more tender, NOT drier (opposite of breast). Hard to overcook.
- **Pan-sear:** skin-side down medium-high until crisp, flip, finish. Best hands-off protein for beginners.
- **Bake/roast:** 425°F ~25–30 min. **Air-fry:** 400°F ~18–22 min, skin gets crispy. **Grill:** medium, ~6–7 min/side. **Boil/braise:** thighs love a braise (simmer in liquid), they only get more tender.
- #1 mistake: pulling at 165 like a breast — give thighs more time, they reward it.

### GROUND MEAT (beef/turkey/pork/chicken)
- **Temp:** 160°F (no pink), poultry-ground 165°F. No rest needed.
- **Pan/sauté (the default):** medium-high, **don't stir constantly** — let it sit to brown (Maillard = flavor), then break up. #1 mistake: crowding a cold pan → it boils grey in its own water instead of browning. Fix: hot pan, work in batches, drain excess liquid.
- **Bake:** meatballs/meatloaf 375–400°F to 160°F. **Not dry:** don't overwork the mix (compacts it tough); leaner blends (93/7) need a binder/moisture (egg, onion, a little broth).

### STEAK (beef)
- **Pull temps (rest 3–5 min, +5°F carryover):** rare 120→125, **medium-rare 130→135 (the sweet spot)**, medium 140→145, well 155+°F. Safe minimum is 145°F + 3 min rest.
- **Pan-sear (cast iron):** pat BONE-DRY (wet = steam, no crust), screaming-hot pan, oil with high smoke point (avocado), ~2–4 min/side, baste with butter at the end. #1 mistake: wet surface + not-hot-enough pan = grey, no sear.
- **Grill:** high direct heat for the sear, move to cooler side to finish thick cuts. **Reverse-sear** thick steaks: low oven ~250°F to 10°F under target, then blast-sear.
- **Not dry/tough:** don't go past medium on lean cuts; **rest** before cutting; **slice against the grain.**

### PORK (chops, loin, tenderloin)
- **Pull temp:** ~140°F, rest 3 min → carryover to the safe **145°F** (USDA lowered pork from 160→145 in 2011 — a little pink is fine & safe). Past ~155°F it dries fast.
- **Pan-sear:** pat dry, medium-high, ~3–4 min/side for a ~1" chop. **Air-fry:** 400°F ~10–12 min, pull at 140°F. **Bake/roast:** tenderloin 400°F ~20 min to 140°F. **Grill:** medium-high, watch closely — loin is lean.
- #1 mistake: cooking pork to "no pink" like the old days → overcooked & chalky. Fix: thermometer, pull at 140°F.

### SALMON (fatty — forgiving)
- **Temp:** USDA 145°F; most cooks pull at **125°F for moist medium** (restaurant standard) up to 145°F well-done. Fatty enough to take bold seasoning.
- **Pan-sear (crispy skin):** pat dry, **skin-side down** in hot oil, press flat 1 sec, 4–6 min until skin crisp & releases on its own, flip 2–4 min. #1 mistake: flipping too early / moving it — skin tears, no crisp.
- **Bake/roast:** 400°F ~12–15 min. **Air-fry:** 390°F ~8–10 min. **Boil/steam:** poach gently in barely-simmering liquid for silky texture. **Grill:** oil the grates, skin-side down first.
- **Not dry:** pull at 125–130°F; it keeps cooking off-heat.

### WHITE FISH (cod, tilapia, halibut — delicate, dries fast)
- **Temp:** 145°F or **until it flakes easily and turns opaque** (the flake test is more reliable than a thermometer in thin fillets).
- **Pan-sear:** pat dry, medium-high, ~3–4 min/side for ~1"; thin fillets cook in minutes. **Bake:** 400°F ~10–15 min. **Air-fry:** 380°F ~8–10 min. **Steam/poach:** ideal for delicate fillets — gentle moist heat won't toughen them. **Grill:** use a basket/foil, it's fragile.
- #1 mistake: overcooking — it goes from done to dry in ~60 seconds. Keep it LIGHT on seasoning (garlic, lemon, dill, a little butter); don't overpower delicate flesh.

### SHRIMP (cooks in ~2–3 min — overcooks instantly)
- **Doneness CUE (better than temp):** **loose "C" shape, pink & opaque = done.** Tight "O" shape + bright white = OVERcooked & rubbery. (~120–125°F internal.)
- **Pan-sear/sauté:** hot pan, single layer, ~1–2 min/side. **Air-fry:** 400°F ~6–8 min. **Boil/steam:** drop in, ~2–3 min until they curl & turn pink, then OUT and into ice water to stop carryover. **Grill:** skewer, ~2 min/side.
- #1 mistake: walking away — they're done in 2–3 min. Pull the instant they curl into a C.

### EGGS
- **Temp:** cook until white & yolk set (over-easy = runny yolk by choice); egg DISHES (frittata/quiche) to 160°F.
- **Scrambled (not rubbery):** **low-and-slow**, pull while still slightly wet & glossy — they finish on the plate. #1 mistake: high heat + cooking until fully dry = rubbery curds.
- **Fried:** medium, lid for set whites/runny yolk. **Hard-boiled (easy-peel):** start in BOILING water (not cold), ~10–11 min, then ICE BATH — peels clean, no grey-green ring (that ring = overcooked). **Soft-boiled:** ~6–7 min. **Poached:** barely-simmering water + a splash of vinegar, swirl, 3–4 min. **Omelet:** medium-low, don't brown.

### TOFU / TEMPEH
- **Crispy tofu (the whole game = remove water):** use **extra-firm**; **press** 20–30 min (wrap in towel under a heavy pan/cans) to squeeze moisture out — wet tofu steams & never crisps. Toss in **cornstarch**, then pan-sear in hot oil **single layer, 3–5 min/side, don't move it** until mahogany and it releases on its own.
- **Air-fry:** 400°F ~15–20 min, shake halfway — easiest crispy method. **Bake:** 400°F ~25–30 min. **Boil/steam:** silken tofu for soups (don't press); steaming firm tofu first then crisping also works.
- **Tempeh:** **steam or simmer 10 min first** to remove its natural bitterness, THEN marinate & pan-sear/bake.
- #1 mistake: skipping the press / crowding the pan → soggy grey tofu. Fix: press hard, hot oil, leave it alone, single layer. Tofu is bland by design — **season aggressively** (it has no flavor of its own).

## GRAINS — so they're fluffy, not mush

### RICE (white, long-grain)
- **Ratio:** ~1 cup rice : 1.5–1.75 cups water (varies by type; brown rice needs more, ~1:2.25 + longer).
- **Method:** rinse until water runs clear (removes surface starch = the #1 cause of gummy/clumpy rice), bring to boil, cover, **drop to LOW simmer**, don't peek/stir, ~15–18 min, then **rest off-heat lid-on 5–10 min**, fluff with a fork.
- #1 mistakes: (1) not rinsing → gluey; (2) lifting the lid / stirring → releases starch & lets steam escape → mush; (3) too much water → soggy.

### QUINOA
- **Ratio:** 1 cup quinoa : **1.75 cups water** (1:2 in a rice cooker). More than this = mush.
- **Method:** **rinse first** (removes bitter saponins — the #1 quinoa complaint), simmer covered ~15 min until water absorbed, then **rest off-heat 5 min** and fluff. Don't stir while cooking (agitation releases starch → gluey).
- #1 mistake: skipping the rinse (bitter) and/or too much water (mushy).

### OATS
- **Rolled (old-fashioned):** ~1:2 oats:liquid, simmer ~5 min. **Steel-cut:** ~1:3–4, simmer ~20–30 min (chewier, less mushy than rolled). **Overnight oats:** ~1:1 oats:liquid, soak cold 6+ hr (no cooking).
- **Not gluey:** don't over-stir steel-cut, and pull rolled oats while still a touch loose — they thicken as they sit. Salt the water; toast oats in a dry pan first for nuttier flavor.

> **General grain rules:** measure the water, **rest covered after cooking** (active starch reorganization = fluffy not wet), and don't stir mid-cook for rice/quinoa.

## VEGETABLES — roast vs steam vs sauté (and how to not make them sad)

### ROAST (best flavor — browning + sweetness)
- **425°F is the sweet spot.** High heat triggers the **Maillard reaction + caramelization** → nutty, sweet, browned. ~20–30 min, toss halfway.
- **#1 mistake = CROWDING the pan.** Vegetables are full of water; packed tight, that water becomes steam and they **steam instead of roast** (soggy, pale, no browning). **Fix: single layer, space around every piece, use two sheet pans if needed.** Also: pat dry, enough oil to coat, don't under-salt.

### STEAM (cleanest, most nutrient-retentive, no browning)
- Gentle moist heat over simmering water until **fork-tender but still bright/vibrant**. Best for delicate veg (broccoli, green beans, asparagus) and when you want them plain/soft.
- #1 mistake: over-steaming → drab, mushy, grey. Pull while still crisp-tender, shock in ice water if serving cold.

### SAUTÉ (fast, some browning, good for aromatics)
- Hot pan + a little oil, keep things moving, high heat. Great for onions/garlic (caramelizes their sugars → flavor base) and quick-cooking veg.
- **#1 mistake = same as roasting: crowding.** Too much in the pan traps moisture → it **steams not sears.** Fix: hot pan, smaller batches, don't overload.

> **One-line rule:** want browning/flavor → roast or sauté at HIGH heat in a SINGLE layer; want clean/soft/nutrient-max → steam and pull early. Crowding is the universal killer (steam, not sear) for both roasting and sautéing.
